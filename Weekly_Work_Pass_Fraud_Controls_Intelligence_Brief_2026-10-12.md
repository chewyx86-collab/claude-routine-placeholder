## 2. Key Developments by Theme

### 2.1 US suspends major H-1B sponsors from PERM in fraud-task-force action
- **Jurisdiction:** US
- **Source:** Spectrum News, 8 Oct 2026 – https://spectrumlocalnews.com/us/snplus/politics/2026/10/08/microsoft-h-1b-visa-fraud-white-house-fraud-task-force ; Boston Globe, 8 Oct 2026 – https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/10/08/nation/microsoft-suspended-green-card-program-trump-officials-widen-fraud-drive/ ; Time, 9 Oct 2026 – https://time.com/article/2026/10/09/us-immigration-visa-fraud-green-card-h1b-j1-microsoft-adobe/ (headline only; page not fetched)
- **What happened:** Per press reports, the VP announced on 8 Oct that Microsoft was suspended from PERM, and reports say Adobe, Cognizant, Infosys, TCS, Wipro, HCL and Capgemini were also covered. DOL reportedly expanded investigations into nine universities over J-1 use allegedly undercutting US wages. The action targets PERM, not existing H-1B status. The stated basis is alleged substitution of US workers (Microsoft layoffs vs. visa filings); Microsoft says most of its H-1B filings are extensions. Duration is unclear ("as long as it needs to"), as are the legal basis and reinstatement conditions. One secondary source (i-9 Intelligence) describes a DOL hold on pending as well as new filings; single-source.
- **Why it matters:** Shows an enforcement model that sanctions *employer-level* behaviour (displacement, filing patterns) by suspending access to a programme, rather than prosecuting individual applications.
- **Implications for MOM-style operations:** Singapore's analogue is employer-level levers (work pass privilege suspension, FCF/Fair Consideration Framework scrutiny, COMPASS). Consider whether employer risk profiles combine hiring-pattern signals (nationality concentration, layoffs of locals vs. pass applications) and whether sanctions can be applied proportionately at employer level pre-prosecution. Treat the US case as policy-signal, not evidence of proven fraud.
- **Watchpoints:** (1) Formal DOL/DHS notice or Federal Register publication clarifying legal basis; (2) litigation or reinstatement terms; (3) whether other jurisdictions mirror displacement-based criteria.

### 2.2 H-1B Visa Fraud Crackdown Act introduced
- **Jurisdiction:** US
- **Source:** Rep. Van Duyne press release, Oct 2026 – https://vanduyne.house.gov/2026/10/van-duyne-introduces-h-1b-visa-fraud-crackdown-act-raising-penalties-for-abuse-in-the-h-1b-visa-program ; Newsweek – https://www.newsweek.com/new-h-1b-bill-employers-250000-fines-12524721 (via search summaries; pages not fetched)
- **What happened:** Bill H.R. 10643 (introduced 1 Oct per search summary) proposes raising civil penalties for willful H-1B violations from US$5,000 to US$100,000, and from US$35,000 to US$250,000 for willful displacement of US workers; reports also mention a possible 10-year ban. Status: introduced and referred to House Judiciary; **policy proposal only**.
- **Why it matters:** Indicates direction of penalty design (deterrence scaled to employer, with a displacement aggravator).
- **Implications:** Useful benchmark when reviewing the proportionality of EFMA penalties for employer-side false declarations; no immediate control change.
- **Watchpoints:** Committee progress; whether penalty aggravators tied to local-worker displacement gain traction.

### 2.3 Canada: AI-impersonation scam warnings and a procedural-fairness ruling
- **Jurisdiction:** Canada
- **Source:** Immigration News Canada summary of IRCC warnings and Federal Court decisions, early Oct 2026 – https://immigrationnewscanada.ca/ ; PTC News – https://www.ptcnews.tv/world-news/ircc-ai-scam-warning-canada-visa-fraud-4430187 ; Immigration News Canada – https://immigrationnewscanada.ca/canada-court-overturns-5-year-immigration-ban/ (secondary reporting; IRCC/court primary pages not verified)
- **What happened:** IRCC (reported 6 Oct) warned of scammers using AI-generated video and voice to pose as officials; another warning (reported 10 Oct) listed red flags including impersonation on social media. Separately, the Federal Court on 1 Oct reportedly set aside a misrepresentation finding carrying a five-year ban because the applicant was not given a fair chance to respond.
- **Why it matters:** The scams target applicants/workers (victim-side fraud), and the ruling shows fraud findings are vulnerable on procedural grounds.
- **Implications:** Ensure public advisories cover AI-enabled impersonation of MOM officers/channels (including fake "MOM" calls, which are a recurring Singapore typology); keep the show-cause/representation step robust and well documented for debarment decisions.
- **Watchpoints:** Official IRCC advisory text; further Federal Court rulings on misrepresentation procedure.

## 4. Emerging Fraud Typologies

- **Employer-side displacement / sponsor gaming (US).** *What changed:* enforcement framed around substitution of local workers and filing patterns. *Indicators:* concentrated hiring from a single source nationality alongside local layoffs; high filing volume relative to headcount changes. *Responses:* employer-level risk scoring using local-workforce movement (CPF data) alongside pass applications.
- **AI-assisted impersonation of officials (Canada).** *Indicators:* unsolicited video/voice contact claiming agency authority, payment demands. *Responses:* public advisory, channel-authentication guidance for employers and workers.
- **Known Singapore typologies (background, not new this week):** phantom-worker CPF contributions to inflate quotas (arrests 31 Mar 2026), job-type misdeclaration with kickbacks (charges 28 May 2026), sham employment for EP holders (conviction 5 Jun 2026), Singpass credential-selling to set up shell companies (earlier charges). See sources in section 8; these are older and flagged as such.

## 6. Implications for MOM-style Operations

- **(a) Pre-approval controls:** No change warranted by this week's evidence. Consider reviewing employer-level displacement/concentration indicators in application risk scoring.
- **(b) Post-approval controls:** US action underscores value of ongoing employer-level monitoring rather than only point-of-application checks; no new evidence on Singapore post-approval gaps this week.
- **(c) Data and analytics:** Cross-referencing local-workforce movements with pass application patterns is the transferable idea.
- **(d) Policy and communications:** Refresh public advisories to cover AI-generated impersonation; keep representation/appeal processes defensible.
