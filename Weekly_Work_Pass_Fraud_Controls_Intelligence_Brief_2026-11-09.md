## 1. Executive Summary

- **Singapore (the one clear in-window item):** On 2 Nov MOM announced it is investigating about 45 companies tied to Changi Airport operations (contractors and subcontractors of ground handler SATS) for suspected false declarations and illegal deployment of foreign workers. About 270 workers are involved so far. Reported typology: Employment Pass holders doing baggage handling, with declared salaries and occupations possibly false. These are investigations, not charges.
- **Control lesson:** The case exposes post-approval deployment risk down a subcontracting chain. MOM has directed SATS to tighten oversight of its contractors, which is a shift toward principal-accountability.
- **US (background, continuing):** The DOL-led H-1B/PERM fraud probe (launched July 2026) escalated in September and October with PERM suspensions of named employers. No new action was confirmed for 2–9 Nov; no charges or formal findings have been announced.
- **Netherlands (effective date in window):** Secondary reporting says new compliance rules for sponsoring employers took effect 1 Nov 2026, including a chance to correct certain administrative violations before fines. Unverified against IND.

## 2. Key Developments by Theme

### 2.1 MOM probes ~45 airport-operations companies over work pass deployment and false declarations
- **Jurisdiction:** Singapore
- **Source:** MOM press release, 2 Nov 2026 ([mom.gov.sg](https://www.mom.gov.sg/newsroom/press-releases/2026/1102-mom-investigating-companies-involved-in-airport-operations-for-suspected-work-pass-offences)); reporting by [Malay Mail](https://www.malaymail.com/news/singapore/2026/11/02/singapore-probes-45-changi-airport-firms-over-suspected-work-pass-offences/241719), [Mothership](https://mothership.sg/2026/11/mom-investigating-companies-airport-operations/), [HCA Mag](https://www.hcamag.com/asia/specialisation/employment-law/mom-probes-45-companies-over-suspected-work-pass-offences-at-changi-airport/595894)
- **What happened:** MOM found Employment Pass holders performing baggage handling during an inspection. SATS says its own routine audit first flagged irregularities among contractors, after which it widened its review and gave MOM a list of contractors and affected workers. Investigations so far point to about 270 workers whose deployment may not match work pass rules; some declared salaries and occupations may have been false. MOM has not named the firms or said how many are direct contractors versus subcontractors, and no timeline has been given. MOM told SATS to tighten oversight of contractors and warned other firms in the operation of their EFMA obligations. Figures come from secondary reports of the MOM release.
- **Why it matters:** It points to an occupation-mismatch typology: high-tier passes (EP) obtained on declared professional roles but used for manual work. This undermines salary/qualifying-criteria controls and the intent of the pass tiers.
- **Implications for MOM-style operations:** Approval-stage checks on declared occupation and salary cannot detect later redeployment, so post-approval verification matters more. Subcontracting chains obscure the real deploying employer. Consider: risk-scoring employers by sector and subcontracting position (aviation, logistics, facilities); cross-checking declared occupation against CPF/payroll patterns and site access or pass data held by principals; and placing contractual and regulatory duties on principals to verify deployment (as MOM has directed SATS).
- **Watchpoints:** (1) Whether MOM names firms or files charges, and the offence mix (false declaration vs illegal deployment). (2) Whether similar principal-contractor reviews are extended to other sectors. (3) Any change to EP/S Pass guidance on deployment duties.

### 2.2 Netherlands: new sponsor compliance rules reported effective 1 Nov 2026
- **Jurisdiction:** Netherlands
- **Source:** Nairametrics, 24 Oct 2026 ([link](https://nairametrics.com/2026/10/24/netherlands-to-introduce-new-rules-for-employers-sponsoring-foreign-workers-from-november-1/)) — secondary source, not verified against IND/government text.
- **What happened:** The report states that new rules for employers sponsoring foreign workers start 1 Nov, and that they allow firms to remedy certain administrative violations before financial penalties apply. Details were not retrievable (fetch blocked). Separately, background from Jan 2026: recognised sponsors must hold proof of actual salary payment (e.g. bank records), not just payslips, per law-firm summaries.
- **Why it matters:** A graduated, cure-before-penalty enforcement model contrasts with the stricter "reasonable suspicion" UK posture, and the salary-payment evidence requirement targets salary-manipulation fraud.
- **Implications:** Worth considering for proportionate enforcement design (separating administrative lapses from fraud) and for evidencing actual salary payment in post-approval checks.
- **Watchpoints:** (1) Confirm the rule text with IND. (2) Whether the cure window excludes false-declaration cases.

### 2.3 Background (not new this week): US H-1B/PERM fraud enforcement
- **Jurisdiction:** US
- **Source:** [Mayer Brown, Sept 2026](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/09/trump-administration-announces-new-enforcement-actions-against-employers-in-h-1b-and-perm-fraud-probes); [Time, 9 Oct 2026](https://time.com/article/2026/10/09/us-immigration-visa-fraud-green-card-h1b-j1-microsoft-adobe/)
- **What is established (pre-window):** DOL OIG announced a nationwide H-1B/PERM probe on 8 Jul 2026 with dozens of subpoenas; Cognizant and Cloudera PERM filings were suspended around 8 Sept; an October announcement extended suspension to eight employers. Law-firm sources note suspension is not revocation and no charges or findings have been announced.
- **What is new this week:** Nothing confirmed in 2–9 Nov.
- **Why it matters / implications:** Illustrates employer-level (not applicant-level) sanctions as a lever, and use of site visits and tips. Monitor for typologies that surface (ghost offices, benching, off-site placement) as they may transfer to Singapore outsourcing/IT-services employers.
- **Watchpoints:** (1) First charges or findings. (2) Formal rules on H-1B selection and wage-based weighting.

## 4. Emerging Fraud Typologies

- **Application gaming / false declarations of occupation and salary (new emphasis this week):** *Changed:* MOM case shows EP holders reportedly used for manual baggage handling. *Indicators:* declared roles inconsistent with sector or site; pass tier inconsistent with observed duties; concentration of passes at subcontractors; CPF/payroll patterns inconsistent with declared salary. *Controls:* post-approval site-based verification, principal-contractor attestation, payroll-to-declaration reconciliation.
- **Sham/phantom employers and phantom workers:** No new case in window; recent Singapore enforcement (March–October 2026 per search results) is background.
- **Other typologies (forged qualifications, synthetic identities, agent abuse, AI-assisted deception):** No in-window development identified.

## 6. Implications for MOM-style Operations

- **(a) Pre-approval controls:** No change warranted by this week's evidence alone. Consider heightened scrutiny of EP applications from firms in sectors with known manual-labour demand where the declared role looks inconsistent with the sector.
- **(b) Post-approval controls:** The Changi case is the main lesson. Verify actual duties and deployment, extend duties to principals over contractor chains, and use trigger-based inspections where sector and pass-tier mix is anomalous.
- **(c) Data and analytics:** Explore employer-network and subcontracting-chain analytics, and reconciliation of declared occupation/salary with CPF contributions and, where lawful, third-party site data.
- **(d) Policy and communications:** Messaging to principals and ground-handling/outsourcing sectors on EFMA obligations; consider whether deployment-oversight duties should be formalised.

## 7. Recommended Follow-up Actions

- **Immediate watchpoints:** MOM naming of firms/charges in the airport case; confirmation of Dutch rule text with IND.
- **Operational review:** Review EP holders in airport, logistics and facilities sectors for occupation/deployment consistency (if not already underway).
- **Data-analytics opportunity:** Pilot a contractor-chain risk indicator.
- **Policy or inter-agency consideration:** Principal-contractor accountability model; none further proposed given the quiet week elsewhere.
